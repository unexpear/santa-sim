# Chimney and fireplace research (for the flue, fire and entry cutscene)

These are real-world anatomy and sizes for a residential masonry chimney with an open fireplace, focused on Australian houses (Federation brick, and the brick chimneys added to Queenslanders). The elf is 0.22 m tall, so nothing needs oversizing: a real flue is roomy for him.

## Soot and creosote

- Creosote condenses where the flue gas cools, so it is worst in the upper flue and in cold external chimneys. Smouldering fires and wet wood make it worse.
- It forms in three stages:
  1. Loose black powdery soot.
  2. Flaky, crunchy tar ("biscuits").
  3. Hard, shiny glaze that runs down the flue in streaks.
- The smoke shelf collects fallen soot and debris. The throat and firebox are baked and lighter: sooty, but with fire-coloured firebrick near the flames.
- In game: the liner is blackest near the top, with glossy creosote runs. Lower down it warms with the fire's glow. The shelf has a crust, and the firebox is buff firebrick, blackened above the fire.

Sources: [FCS creosote guide](https://findchimneysweepers.com/resources/creosote-guide), [University of Idaho WFS6](https://www.uidaho.edu/-/media/UIdaho-Responsive/Files/Extension/topic/forestry/WFS6-The-Creosote-Problem-Chimney-Fires-and-Chimney-Cleaning.pdf), [CSL smoke shelf](https://www.cleanyourchimney.com/tips.html)

## What it means for the descent (0.22 m elf)

The 250 mm liner is snug: shoulders fit with a hand's width to spare.

1. He abseils down the tight sooty liner.
2. The smoke chamber opens out below him in stepped brick courses.
3. He squeezes past the damper plate beside the smoke shelf.
4. He drops through the throat into the firebox and the fire glow.
